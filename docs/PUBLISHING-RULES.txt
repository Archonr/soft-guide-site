SOFT GUIDE — WHAT THE PUBLIC SITE MAY SAY

This repository is public and every push to main publishes softguide.dev.
Treat every line here as published, including drafts and commit messages.

THE TEST

A page says what we deliver and why it matters. It does not say how we
build it. Before publishing, ask: could a competent engineer rebuild this
from the page alone? If yes, cut until the answer is no.

PUBLISH

- The problem, in the reader's words, and what it costs them.
- The outcome and the guarantees we stand behind ("nothing changes without
  an approval", "an account inherits the guardrails on day one").
- Principles and trade-offs at the level of a conference talk abstract.
- Widely known open-source tools by name (Kubernetes, Flux, Cilium, Envoy,
  Terraform) where naming them builds trust.
- Packages, process, what the client receives, how an engagement runs.
- The operations autonomy levels as a model, without the gates between
  them.

DO NOT PUBLISH

- Code, configuration, policy text, commands, test fixtures, diagrams
  labelled with real resource, label, file or repository names.
- Numbers that are part of the method: thresholds, gates, time windows,
  limits, counts, quotas, ratios.
- Step-by-step sequences of how something is built, rolled out or tested.
- The exact composition of the agent stack: which runtime, sandbox,
  chat integration, behaviour kit or access broker, and how they connect.
- Internal component names, repository layout, renderer or packaging
  mechanics, upstream patches.
- Open questions, known gaps, risks and weaknesses of our own designs.
- Client, employer, partner-project or person names; account IDs, IPs,
  hostnames, domains other than our own.
- Competitor names and comparisons.

WHERE THE DETAIL LIVES

Method, numbers and designs stay in the private repositories and are shared
with a client under contract. The site points to a conversation, not a
recipe.

ENFORCEMENT

tests/site.test.mjs fails a build whose notes contain code blocks, IP
addresses, ARNs, template variables or locally listed private terms.
A human still reads every page against this file before a release.
